Dievo Apvaizdos — Divine Providence Lithuanian Catholic Church — is the last Lithuanian Catholic parish in the Detroit area. It stands in Southfield, at Beech Daly and Nine Mile Roads, with a cultural center beside the church that holds a Lithuanian school, choir, archive, dance groups, scout troops, ateitininkai, the Kovas sports club, the Lithuanian Daughters, and the Lithuanian Community. It is a personal parish under canon law, established by reason of language and nationality; 65% of its members live outside the parish boundary, driving in from across metropolitan Detroit, from Grand Rapids, and from Canada. The Archdiocese of Detroit is now deciding the future of all 209 of its parishes, this one among them.

#### Three churches

Detroit’s Lithuanians founded their first parish in 1908 — St. George’s, at Westminster and Cardoni Streets — one of the oldest continuously operating Lithuanian parishes in the United States. In 1948 the parish took the name Divine Providence and moved to Schaefer and Grand River as its people moved. Highway construction took that church, and by the late 1960s the community faced building for a third time. Its leaders decided that if they were going to build again, they would first secure the parish’s standing in writing: a Lithuanian parish, with no territorial boundaries.

#### In writing

In August 1967 the parish committee sent that request to the Detroit chancery. On December 2, 1968, a large gathering of parishioners met with the newly consecrated auxiliary bishop, Thomas J. Gumbleton. The parish’s anniversary book records the meeting candidly: the bishop urged them to accept an ordinary territorial parish; the younger generation and the Knights of Lithuania argued for the Lithuanian one; the committee chair, Antanas Dainius, called a vote, and it was unanimous. The bishop closed the meeting with the words, “It will be as you wish.”

The letter arrived on December 31, 1968, from the Office of the Vicar for Parishes, written on behalf of Archbishop John Dearden. It accepted the site at Beech Daly and Nine Mile and the parishioners’ decision: Divine Providence would be a national parish, where only Lithuanians register and the Lithuanians themselves provide the support.

The community held up its side entirely. It bought the Southfield land from the Archdiocese, raised the money itself, and built the church, rectory, and cultural center without touching the construction loan the Archdiocese had approved. The church — its stained glass by Vytautas Kazimieras Jonynas, its woodcarvings by Jurgis Daugvila — was consecrated on September 8, 1973, the same year the Soviet regime was bulldozing the Hill of Crosses in occupied Lithuania. Five years later the pastor, Fr. Viktoras Kriščiūnevičius, wrote in the parish’s anniversary book: “A good number of our parishes are fading as their surroundings change… Our parish, too, may one day face such trials.”

#### A new wave

When Lithuania regained its independence in 1990, after fifty years of Soviet rule, a new generation came to America. Divine Providence became, in the parish’s own words, “a beacon of hope” and a sense of “home” for the newcomers. They became cantors and lectors, joined the Bible-study and pilgrimage groups, and brought their children to the Saturday Lithuanian school — sixty children when the parish described itself to the Archbishop in 2020. During the pandemic, the parish’s live-streamed Sunday Masses were viewed by thousands each week.

#### The last one

The parishes around Divine Providence closed one by one. St. Anthony (Šv. Antano) in Detroit survived a fire in 1989, when Cardinal Edmund Szoka chose to rebuild it; it closed in 2013 and was merged into Divine Providence. Since then, Divine Providence has been the last Lithuanian Catholic parish in the Detroit area.

#### What is happening now

The parish has been through this before. When the Families of Parishes reorganization began, the parish wrote to Archbishop Vigneron in June 2020 asking that Divine Providence not be clustered with other parishes: Lithuania’s bishops supply it a priest, it runs at no financial burden to the Archdiocese, and its mission — serving Lithuanian Catholics in Lithuanian — is one no other parish can carry.

In March 2026 the Archdiocese published a restructuring workbook for each of its 209 parishes, and then three draft models for Divine Providence’s planning area. Two of the three would leave the parish without a regular weekend Mass. The parish’s own workbook shows over $1 million in unrestricted cash and effectively zero debt. The public-feedback window closed on July 31; final models are expected in April 2027, and implementation begins July 1, 2027. No decree merging or suppressing the parish, or relegating the church to profane use, has been issued.

The record of dialogue so far is short. Archbishop Lionginas Virbalas, SJ, the Lithuanian Bishops’ Conference delegate for Lithuanian Catholics abroad, offered to provide a Lithuanian priest at the parish’s own expense; Archbishop Weisenburger declined the proposal. Lithuania’s Ambassador Gediminas Varvuolis sought a meeting during his June visit, and the Archdiocese declined to meet with him. Archbishop Virbalas visited in July and likewise was not granted a meeting. The parish has been without a resident pastor since Fr. Tomas Miliauskas, MIC, concluded his service in May 2026.

The parish’s life continues. Its registers from 2022 through 2026 record 12 baptisms, 20 First Communions, 32 Confirmations, and 5 marriages. This fall at least ten children are preparing for First Communion and Confirmation, and the re-formed youth choir will sing at Mass. The scouts have served Detroit’s Lithuanian youth for seventy-five years under the motto *Dievui, Tėvynei, Artimui* — for God, for Country, for Neighbor. The Šaltinis dancers have just returned from the North American Lithuanian Folk Dance Festival in Chicago, where more than 2,000 dancers gathered. The church itself is on Lithuania’s official list of cultural heritage significant to Lithuania in the United States, under the 2002 cultural-property agreement between the two countries.
